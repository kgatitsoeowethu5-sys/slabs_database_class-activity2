[SuperMart SA: Enterprise Information Systems Mapping]


1. Transaction Processing System (TPS)

SuperMart SA uses a Transaction Processing System (TPS) to record daily business transactions accurately and reliably.

- Checkout counters: Record sales, scan product barcodes, calculate totals, process payments, issue receipts, and update stock quantities.
- Inventory warehouse: Record deliveries from suppliers, goods received, stock transfers, items dispatched to stores, returned products, and damaged or missing stock.

The TPS helps ensure that transactions are recorded correctly and that inventory records remain up to date.


2. Management Information Systems (MIS) and Decision Support Systems (DSS)

- Management Information System (MIS)

Store management uses an MIS to generate weekly sales reports showing total sales, best-selling products, slow-moving items, stock levels, and performance compared with previous weeks.

These reports help managers monitor performance, identify problems, and make informed operational decisions.

- Decision Support System (DSS)

If sales suddenly drop, a DSS can analyse sales history, product prices, customer demand, stock availability, and competitor promotions.

Managers can use this information to compare possible actions, such as offering discounts, running a special promotion, or advertising selected products. The DSS helps them estimate which option may improve sales while protecting profits.




3. Executive Support System (ESS)

The CEO of SuperMart SA uses an Executive Support System (ESS) to view high-level information about the company's overall performance and long-term direction.

The ESS can provide:

- High-level reports: Total revenue, profit margins, operating costs, and performance across all stores.
- Competitor data: Competitors' prices, promotions, market share, and business strategies.
- Long-term trend forecasts: Predicted sales growth, customer demand, market trends, and future business risks.
- Strategic decision support: Information to help decide whether to open new stores, invest in technology, or expand into new markets.

This information helps the CEO make strategic decisions that support the company's long-term success.




4. Knowledge Work Systems (KWS) and Office Automation Systems (OAS)

- Knowledge Work System (KWS)

SuperMart SA's designers could use computer-aided design (CAD) software to create store layouts, plan shelf arrangements, and design promotional displays.

This helps designers develop and evaluate ideas before implementing them in stores.

-Office Automation System (OAS)

SuperMart SA employees could use Microsoft Outlook to send emails, schedule meetings, and manage calendars.

This improves communication and helps employees coordinate daily administrative tasks.



5. IT Landscape and Legacy Systems

- Why connecting legacy systems to modern cloud applications increases complexity

SuperMart SA has used different software systems over 15 years. Older legacy systems may use outdated technologies, incompatible data formats, and older communication methods.

Connecting these systems to modern cloud applications can be complex because:

- Compatibility issues: Older software may not communicate directly with modern cloud applications.
- Data integration challenges: Different systems may store information in different formats.
- Security risks: Older systems may lack modern security features.
- Maintenance difficulties: Integration requires additional technical support, testing, and monitoring.
- Higher costs: The company may need special connectors, software upgrades, or replacement systems.

An IT Landscape Diagram helps SuperMart SA visualise its systems, connections, dependencies, and potential areas for improvement.




- Two ways SuperMart SA can save costs using an IT Landscape Diagram

1. End-of-Life (EOL) asset tracking

The IT Landscape Diagram can identify outdated hardware and software that no longer receive vendor support or security updates. SuperMart SA can prioritise replacing or retiring these assets before they cause expensive failures, security incidents, or maintenance problems.

This reduces unexpected repair costs and helps the company plan its technology budget.

2. Standardisation of systems

The diagram can reveal departments or stores using multiple applications that perform the same function. SuperMart SA can standardise on fewer approved systems instead of paying for several overlapping software licences and support contracts.

This reduces licensing, training, maintenance, and support costs while making it easier to manage the company's IT environment.




- Conclusion

Enterprise information systems help SuperMart SA record daily transactions, monitor business performance, support decisions, and plan long-term growth. Mapping the IT landscape also helps the company understand its technology dependencies, manage legacy systems, reduce costs, and improve efficiency.
